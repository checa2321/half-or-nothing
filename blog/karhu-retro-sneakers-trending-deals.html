Why Karhu Is Suddenly Everywhere

If you have seen a chunky, slightly technical-looking running sneaker in beige, sage, or dusty blue popping up on your feed lately, there is a decent chance it was a Karhu. The Finnish brand has been quietly building a following among people who want the retro running look without wearing the same three sneakers everyone else already owns.

A Real Olympic Running Brand, Not a Reissue Gimmick

Karhu was founded in Helsinki in 1916, originally making javelins, skis, and discuses out of birch before moving into footwear. The brand name means "bear" in Finnish. Its running credentials are not marketing copy. At the 1920 Antwerp Olympics, Finnish athletes swept all three medals in the javelin using Karhu equipment, and the country's runners, nicknamed the "Flying Finns," took five gold medals wearing Karhu spikes.

In 1924, Karhu became the official Olympic equipment supplier, the same year Paavo Nurmi, still considered one of the greatest middle-distance runners ever, won five gold medals at the Paris Games. Nurmi wore all-white Karhu running shoes made from kangaroo leather at a time when every other competitor raced in black spikes. Over his career he brought home nine Olympic golds and set 22 world records, most of them in Karhu shoes. Karhu's Olympic run continued into the 1952 Helsinki Games, where the brand's shoes were on the podium for fifteen gold medal performances, three of them belonging to Emil Zatopek.

The Adidas Story

Here is the piece of trivia most people do not know: those three stripes on Adidas sneakers did not start at Adidas. Karhu owned that mark first and sold it to Adidas in 1951 for a small cash payment and, as the story is usually told, two bottles of whiskey. Adidas turned it into one of the most recognized logos in sportswear. Karhu kept the money, kept making shoes, and later registered its own M logo, short for "Mestari," Finnish for "champion."

From Running Shoe to Streetwear Fixture

Karhu spent decades as a respected but low-profile performance brand before a new ownership group leaned into its archive in the 2010s, reissuing models like the Fusion 2.0 and Aria 95 as retro lifestyle sneakers. The turning point for US recognition came in 2017, when Kanye West was photographed wearing a pair of Fusion 2.0s, which sent resale interest and search volume up almost overnight. Since then, Karhu has picked up real sneaker-world collaborations rather than just riding nostalgia: Boston sneaker boutique Concepts relaunched the Aria 95 in a Finland-inspired colorway, Amsterdam's Patta put out its own Fusion 2.0 pairing, Sneakersnstuff released a Moomin-patterned Albatross, and British workwear label Universal Works has become a recurring collaborator on the Trampas silhouette, one of which is in this list.

That combination, a genuine Olympic-era running pedigree plus a low-key cult following in sneaker culture, is exactly what is fueling the current wave of interest in Karhu silhouettes like the Legacy 96, Fusion XC, and Aria 95.

Which Karhu Should You Get

All six families below trace back to real running silhouettes, but they wear differently. Here is how they compare if you are choosing between them.

  • Fusion 2.0: The most casual of the bunch. A low-profile 1996 lifestyle shoe, leather or suede upper, closer to a classic runner than anything technical. Best if you want the archival Karhu look, the one Kanye West was wearing.
  • Fusion XC: The same cushioned platform as the Fusion 2.0, but with a technical mesh upper and a chunkier outsole built for light trail use. Best if you want the "gorpcore" outdoor-adjacent look that's part of this whole trend.
  • Legacy 96: The shoe most people picture when they hear "Karhu." A running-inspired lifestyle sneaker with the brand's signature curved side panel. The safest, most versatile pick if you only want one pair.
  • Aria 95: Rounder and softer than the Legacy 96, closer to a classic 90s trainer silhouette. Best for a slightly dressier, less chunky everyday shoe.
  • Synchron Classic: Karhu's orienteering trainer, originally built for cross-country runners. Reads more sporty and technical than the others on this list.
  • Karhu x Universal Works Trampas: The only true designer collaboration here rather than a heritage reissue. Best if you want something a step more fashion-forward and harder to find on someone else's feet.
