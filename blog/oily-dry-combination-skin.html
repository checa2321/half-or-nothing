Oily, Dry, or Combination Skin: How to Know Your Type and What to Use

How to find out if your skin is oily or dry

Dermatologists generally sort skin into five types: normal, oily, dry, combination, and sensitive. The American Academy of Dermatology and Cleveland Clinic both use two simple checks to place you.

The bare-face test. Wash with a mild cleanser, apply nothing else, and wait 30 minutes. Tightness when you make a facial expression points to dry skin. An all-over shine points to oily skin.

The blotting sheet test. Press a clean blotting sheet against different areas of your face and hold it to the light. Loaded with oil means oily. Nearly clean plus any tightness or flaking means dry. Oil from the nose and forehead only, clear from the cheeks, means combination.

Day to day, oily skin looks shiny within a few hours of washing and breaks out more, dry skin feels tight and flakes, and combination does both by zone. Two types are less about oil: normal skin stays balanced, and sensitive skin reacts easily and can sit on top of any other type. There is a short section on both further down.

Skin type also shifts with the seasons and hormones, so re-check once or twice a year. If you are still not sure, a board-certified dermatologist can tell you.

Is combination skin real?

Yes, and it is common. In an international survey of 14,317 women across nine countries published in Acta Dermato-Venereologica in 2024, 39.1% described their skin as combination, more than any single other type. In the same survey, 46.1% said their skin was sensitive.

Combination skin means the T-zone (forehead, nose, and chin) runs oily while the cheeks run normal to dry, sometimes flaky. The same study found that about 45% of women with dry skin and 40% with oily skin said their skin left them lacking confidence, which is why a routine that fits your skin is worth getting right.

The approach is to treat the two zones with different weights of product. Use a gentle cleanser on the whole face, put lighter oil-free products where you are shiny and richer creams where you are tight, exfoliate once or twice a week, and spot-treat the T-zone if it breaks out.

If your skin is oily: what to use

The goal is to clear excess oil without stripping the skin. The American Academy of Dermatology warns that a cleanser that is too harsh irritates the skin and can trigger more oil. A 2025 Delphi consensus of cosmetic dermatologists in the Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology put salicylic acid and retinoids at the top for oily skin.

Cleanse twice a day and after sweating. Use a gentle foaming or gel cleanser, and do not scrub. A formula with salicylic acid works inside the pore on the plugs that cause congestion. At night, a micellar water made for oily skin lifts off makeup and sunscreen first.

If your skin is dry: what to use

The goal is to add water back to the skin and seal it in. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends short warm showers, a gentle fragrance-free cleanser, and moisturizer applied while the skin is still damp. Dry skin does best with humectants that pull in water, like glycerin and hyaluronic acid, under richer ingredients that hold it in, like ceramides and shea butter.

Cleanse gently, or just rinse with water in the morning. Use a mild, fragrance-free cleanser or a nourishing cleansing bar. Skip anything that foams hard or leaves the skin squeaky.

What to skip when the skin is very dry: products with alcohol or fragrance, and retinol on the nights your skin feels raw. Add those back once the barrier feels normal again. For makeup, choose hydrating or dewy formulas over matte ones.

Normal and sensitive skin

Normal skin is balanced, not tight and not shiny, with few breakouts. The routine is short: a gentle cleanser, a light moisturizer, and broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher every day. Add a treatment like vitamin C or a retinol only if you have a specific goal.

Sensitive skin is about how easily your skin reacts, not how much oil it makes, so it can overlap oily, dry, or combination. It stings, flushes, or breaks out from fragrance, dyes, alcohol, and strong actives. Keep it minimal: a fragrance-free cleanser and moisturizer, a mineral sunscreen, and one new product at a time with a few days between each. If redness or stinging is constant, see a dermatologist so rosacea or eczema can be ruled out.

What every skin type should do

Wear sunscreen every day. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher for everyone, regardless of skin type or tone. Oily skin does well with gel and fluid textures. Dry skin can use a moisturizing or cream SPF. The Clevariel SPF 50 above suits both.

Do not over-exfoliate. More is not better. The American Academy of Dermatology notes that exfoliation is not right for every skin type, and that dry, sensitive, or acne-prone skin may only tolerate a soft washcloth with a mild chemical exfoliant. Signs you have pushed too hard: redness, stinging when you apply products, new tightness or flaking, and breakouts that were not there before. If that happens, stop all acids and retinol and go back to a gentle cleanser and moisturizer for a week or two.

Patch test and give it time. Try a new active on the inside of your forearm for a few days first. Most skincare changes take four to twelve weeks to show, so change one thing at a time.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my skin is oily or dry?

Wash with a gentle cleanser, wait 30 minutes, and put nothing else on. Tight or flaky leans dry. Shiny across the forehead, nose, and chin leans oily. A blotting sheet is a second check: heavy with oil means oily, nearly clean plus tightness means dry.

Does oily skin need moisturizer?

Yes. The American Academy of Dermatology says it is still important to moisturize oily skin, just with a lightweight, oil-free, non-comedogenic formula. Skipping moisturizer can leave the skin dehydrated, which can make it produce even more oil.

Can my skin be both oily and dry at the same time?

Yes, that is combination skin, and it is the most common type. The T-zone runs oily while the cheeks run normal to dry. Use lighter, oil-free products where you are shiny and richer, more hydrating products where you are tight.

How often should I use a clay mask or an exfoliant?

Once a week for a clay mask is enough for most oily skin. For a leave-on exfoliant, start with two or three times a week and see how your skin responds. Dry and sensitive skin should exfoliate less, or use only a soft washcloth with a mild chemical exfoliant.
