A quick note on what "dupe" means here. In this guide, "dupe" means a separately sold fragrance with a documented, recognizable scent comparison. It does not mean an identical formula or a counterfeit. Each of the six is its own perfume, sold under its own name and brand, built around a similar scent profile. A "smells like" claim documents a recognizable similarity in notes and structure. It is not a legal claim and not an insult to the original.

Before You Blind Buy

Perfume is personal. A documented comparison means two fragrances share a direction. It does not mean they will smell the same on every person's skin. If you are unsure, try a decant first.

  • Easiest to blind buy: Yara Tous, Ana Abiyedh Scarlet, and Pride Art of Wood.
  • For something less sweet: Pride Art of Wood.
  • For a richer, nuttier gourmand: Eclaire Pistache.
  • For a warm caramel gourmand: Zimaya Tiramisu Caramel.
  • For someone who already loves marshmallow gourmands: Odyssey Marshmallow.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is buying a perfume dupe the same as buying a fake?

No. A dupe is a separate fragrance sold under its own brand name and formula. A counterfeit is an illegal copy sold under the original brand's own name and packaging.

Do these dupes smell exactly like the original?

Not exactly. Where reviewers note a real difference, we call it out. Eclaire Pistache reads richer and toastier than Kayali's pistachio. Odyssey Marshmallow reads less creamy than Yum Boujee Marshmallow.

Is it safe to blind buy a perfume dupe?

It depends on the scent family. A rose, tropical, or citrus-wood profile is easier to predict. A more specific profile, like a marshmallow gourmand, is a bigger gamble if you have never worn that family. We give our take on each one above.

Why are so many of these dupes from Lattafa?

Four of the six picks here are Lattafa, a house with a large budget catalog that shows up in a lot of "smells like" discussions. For more on the houses behind these bottles, read our guide to niche Arabic perfume brands.
