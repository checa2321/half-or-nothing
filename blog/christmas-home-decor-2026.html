From Dann

There’s nothing quite like Christmas. Seriously, it’s my favorite time of the year, and I’m not even going to pretend otherwise. By November 1st, my house is usually already decorated, the tree is up, the lights are on, and I’m probably still finding little things I want to add. Because apparently, there’s always room for one more Christmas decoration. Ahahaha!

I love everything about it. The lights, the colors, the scents, and all those little details that make every corner feel special. But my favorite part? The evenings. There’s nothing better than a cozy night with the house smelling amazing, the Christmas lights glowing, and, as always, a good book in my hands. Honestly, give me that and I could happily forget about the rest of the world for a few hours.

So, putting this blog together was probably one of the easiest decisions I’ve made. I wanted to share some of the things I love most about decorating for Christmas, from the tree and ornaments to the garlands, ribbons, and all those cozy little touches that make a house feel like Christmas. And, of course, since this is Half or Nothing, every find here was at least 50% off when I checked.

Whether you're the type who starts decorating in October, waits until after Thanksgiving, or just needs one more excuse to buy another ornament, I hope you find something here that makes you as happy as Christmas makes me.

Find Your Christmas Style

Designers do not agree on one look this year, and that is good news. Christmas Central names five style trends for 2026: the Ralph Lauren look, Williamsburg Christmas, Natural Christmas, Merry Maximalism and Cozy Christmas Escapes. Homes & Gardens points to six themes, from whimsical to rustic country, and says bows are back on trees, tabletops and mantels. Ideal Home lists heritage tartan and XL bows among its ten trends.

The newest roundups say much the same thing. House Beautiful's 10 biggest Christmas trends for 2026, updated on November 5, runs from bigger, bolder bows to a tinsel comeback, vintage Christmas looks, velvet chain garlands, and ceramic houses and miniature Christmas villages for the mantel. Homes & Gardens' six trends for the 2026 season are Victoriana, Ralph Lauren Heritage, Whimsical Nostalgia, Folk, Kitschmas and Pared-Back Minimalism. You do not have to pick one. The Tree Edit

Start with the tree, because everything else is built around it. Both of these come with the lights already on the branches, which saves you an afternoon of untangling strings.

Before you click, measure the spot where the tree will live: the floor space and the height up to the ceiling. Leave room for a tree collar or skirt and a little breathing space so you can reach every branch. Once it is out of the box, take a few minutes to fluff each branch. It is the difference between a tree that looks sparse and one that looks full.

The Ornament Story

Mix finishes. A few glass pieces, a bronze and copper set and a box of minis already read as a collected tree. A simple way to hang them: put the biggest ornaments deeper in the tree first, then the medium ones, and finish with the small ones near the tips so the light catches them. Keep the keepsake-style pieces, like the glass and the Holy Family, at eye level where people will actually see them.

Make It Look Expensive

You do not need to buy everything new. Three habits do most of the work:

  • Keep the palette short. Pick two or three colors and repeat them on the tree, the mantel and the table.
  • Mix textures. Velvet next to glass next to cedar looks collected. All one material looks flat.
  • Let one thing be the star. A big bow, a tall tree or a long garland. Everything else supports it.

Small Space? Start Here

A slim tree, a garland across the mantel and a few taper candles give a small room the full Christmas feel without crowding it. Pillow covers are the cheapest way to change a sofa.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Christmas decor styles are trending in 2026?

Heritage looks are strong. Christmas Central, Homes & Gardens and Ideal Home all point to Ralph Lauren-style tradition, bows and tartan, with whimsical and handmade looks alongside them.

Should I buy a pre-lit or an unlit tree?

Pre-lit saves time because the lights are already on the branches. Choose unlit only if you want to place your own lights.

Warm white or multicolor lights?

Warm white suits heritage and natural looks. Multicolor suits the whimsical and retro looks.

Can I burn a fall scent in December?

Yes. Scents are personal, and a warm pumpkin candle can sit next to a cranberry one. Read the notes on the product page and pick what you like.

How do I choose the right tree size?

Measure the floor space and the height up to the ceiling, then leave room for a collar or skirt and for walking around the tree. A slim tree suits a corner or a smaller room, and a taller, fuller one suits a room with the ceiling height to carry it.

Which Christmas style should I pick?

Pick the one that feels like your home. If you love tradition, go heritage with velvet, glass and warm lights. If you like calm, go natural with greenery, cream and wicker. If you like fun, go playful with the unexpected ornaments and plenty of lights. Mixing two is completely fine.
