Where to Buy Discounted Electronics and Gadgets (50% Off or More, Verified Live)

2026-09-14 · Written by Checarlos Cruz · 11 min read

Most "electronics deals" online are not real deals. A 20% discount on a product that was quietly marked up two weeks ago is a discount in name only. The real question isn't "is this on sale?" It's "is this actually half the normal price?"

That's the bar that matters. Once you set it at 50% off, most sources drop out. Retail stores rarely go that deep outside of clearance. Brand websites almost never do. Only a short list of marketplaces consistently hit that number, and knowing which ones to trust is what this guide is for.

Key Takeaways

  • Large peer-to-peer marketplaces like eBay produce genuine 50%+ electronics discounts because individual sellers, not a corporate margin floor, set the price.
  • Check a seller's feedback score, feedback recency, and return policy before buying, not just the discount percentage.
  • Open-box and seller-refurbished listings carry the deepest discounts, and the risk is manageable with a few minutes of research.
  • Headphones, phones and accessories, and computers depreciate fast and discount often; current-gen consoles and Apple hardware rarely hit 50% off.
  • eBay's Money Back Guarantee covers most purchases if an item doesn't arrive or doesn't match the listing.

Which Marketplaces Actually Produce 50%+ Electronics Discounts

The short answer: big peer-to-peer and liquidation marketplaces, not brand or retail sites. Here's why each type performs differently once you set the bar at 50%.

Large Peer-to-Peer Marketplaces (eBay)

eBay is the most reliable source of real 50%+ electronics discounts. Its prices come from individual sellers competing against each other, not from one company's margin target. A seller clearing out storage, liquidating stock, or reselling open-box returns has no reason to hold the price up. That's why electronics on eBay often list at 50%, 60%, even 80% off.

The catch is volume. eBay has millions of electronics listings live at any moment. Finding the real deals means filtering hard by discount size, seller reputation, and condition. That's the exact problem Half or Nothing solves: it filters eBay's electronics inventory down to just the listings at 50% off or more, and keeps that list updated around the clock.

Liquidation and Overstock Channels

Extra stock from manufacturers, returns from other retailers, and last-year's models often end up on eBay before they reach anyone else. You'll see them under labels like "open box," "seller refurbished," or "for parts/not working." Each label carries its own risk and its own discount range, covered below.

Retail Clearance (Narrow Window)

Physical stores do go 50%+ off electronics sometimes, but only in short windows: when a new model launches, when a store closes, or right after the holidays. Those windows are brief and hard to predict. Treating retail clearance as your main strategy means checking dozens of stores on no fixed schedule, with no guarantee you'll find what you need.

The practical takeaway: for steady access to 50%+ electronics discounts without hours of manual searching, eBay listings filtered by discount size are the most reliable channel available right now.

How to Evaluate Retailer and Seller Reputation Before You Buy

A deep discount means nothing if the seller can't be trusted. This worry comes up constantly with new marketplace shoppers, and it's a fair one. Here's what to check.

On eBay Specifically

eBay's feedback system gives you a direct read on how reliable a seller is. Before buying any electronics listing, check:

  • Feedback score and percentage: Look for at least 98% positive feedback and 50 or more completed sales. Sellers with thousands of sales and 99%+ ratings are the safest tier.
  • Feedback recency: A seller with 500 good reviews from three years ago is a different risk than one with 200 good reviews in the last six months.
  • Return policy: eBay's Money Back Guarantee covers most purchases, but a seller who also offers their own returns gives you an extra layer of protection.
  • Listing detail: Trustworthy sellers describe condition clearly. A vague line like "works great" with no mention of cosmetic wear or missing accessories is a warning sign.

What "Top Rated Seller" Actually Means

To earn eBay's Top Rated Seller badge, a seller has to hold 98%+ positive feedback, ship on time on at least 95% of orders, and keep disputes under 0.3%. It's not a guarantee, but it cuts real risk on a high-value electronics purchase.

The Open-Box Case

"Open box" is the most confusing label out there. It can mean a customer opened it and returned it right away, basically new, or it can mean someone used it for months before sending it back with real wear. The discount reflects that gap in the unknown.

On eBay, a seller who writes specific notes ("screen protector applied, no scratches, all accessories included") is usually more trustworthy than one who just slaps on the label with no detail. Tom's Guide's electronics buying advice always recommends reading the full description on any open-box item instead of trusting the label alone.

Refurbished Is Not a Red Flag

"Refurbished" has a bad reputation it doesn't deserve for most electronics. A refurbished laptop or headphone set that's been tested, fixed if needed, and confirmed working is often a smarter buy than a new budget model at the same price. What matters is who did the work: manufacturer-certified programs come with a real warranty, while seller-refurbished quality varies a lot from seller to seller.

Bottom line: the deepest discounts on electronics live in open-box and seller-refurbished listings. A few minutes of checking the seller, plus eBay's buyer protections, makes the risk manageable.

How Fulfillment Works When Buying Discounted Electronics Online

Most guides skip what happens after you click buy. Here's what actually happens at each step, and what to expect.

Shipping on eBay Listings

eBay sellers ship on their own, so handling time and carriers vary seller to seller. Most electronics sellers worth buying from offer tracked shipping and list their handling time up front. Standard handling runs one to three business days; some high-volume sellers ship the same day.

When you're buying discounted electronics, check:

1. The handling time on the listing. "3-5 business days" means that's before it even ships, not the full delivery time.

2. Carrier and tracking. Tracked shipping is standard for electronics. If a high-value listing only offers untracked shipping, that's worth thinking twice about.

3. The delivery window at checkout. eBay estimates delivery based on the seller's location and handling time. It's usually accurate for shipments within the US.

Returns and Buyer Protection

eBay's Money Back Guarantee covers most purchases: if an item never shows up or doesn't match the listing, you get a refund or replacement. eBay's resolution center handles the process, not the seller directly.

For electronics, "not as described" is the most common reason for a return. That's why listing detail matters so much: a seller who describes condition accurately gives themselves less room for a dispute, and a buyer who actually reads the description has a stronger case if something's wrong.
