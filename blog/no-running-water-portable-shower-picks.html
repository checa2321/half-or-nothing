4 Portable Showers for Puerto Rico's Water Crisis, Compared

Published 2026-08-27 · Updated 2026-08-31 · Written by Checarlos Cruz · 9 min read

I love Puerto Rico. I miss it. And it's genuinely brutal to watch what's happening there right now with la crisis del agua.

For the past several years, the story out of the island has mostly been about the power grid and LUMA Energy, the private utility that runs it, and that problem hasn't gone away. But this year it's water too. Not “the water tastes off” or “pressure is low.” Hundreds of thousands of people, mostly in the San Juan metro area, have gone days at a time, in some cases over a year, without water reliably coming out of the tap. That's not supposed to be a thing anyone has to plan their week around, and it makes me sick that my people are dealing with it.

What's actually happening

This isn't exaggerated for effect, the numbers are public and they're bad. Puerto Rico Governor Jenniffer González declared a state of emergency in late July 2026 as the Carraízo reservoir (embalse Carraízo), which feeds much of the San Juan metro area, dropped to critical water levels (CBS News, 2026). San Juan recorded its driest July since record-keeping began, according to National Weather Service data cited by Diario Libre. 17% of the island is in extreme drought, and more than half is under at least severe drought conditions.

On August 7, 2026, rationing (el racionamiento) began: 48 hours with water, then 48 hours without, for roughly 183,000 accounts, more than half a million people, across San Juan, Carolina, Juncos, Gurabo, Trujillo Alto, Canóvanas, and Loíza, per the same Diario Libre report. The National Guard was activated back in June just to distribute drinking water to families going days, sometimes weeks, without service (NPR, 2026). Plenty of people are stuck buying bottled water at the supermercado on the “off” days too, since low-income households in particular can't just wait out a 48-hour stretch with nothing.

There's a small silver lining as of this week: Tropical Storm Dolly formed in the Atlantic, weakened into a tropical wave, and brought rain across Puerto Rico from August 29 into the 31st. Meteorologists called it a rare beneficial tropical storm, since a weak, no-flooding system dropping real rain is close to a best-case scenario for reservoir levels this deep into a drought (Washington Post; Yale Climate Connections, 2026). One storm doesn't undo a drought this bad, though, and the rationing schedule hasn't changed.

Where this fits on Half or Nothing

We're a deals site, not a relief organization, and I'm not going to pretend a $30 gadget fixes a water crisis. But when I saw how many people are stuck bucket-bathing or worse during the “off” 48 hours, it made me actually look into portable showers (what a lot of people are searching for as duchas portátiles) instead of just half-registering they exist. If you or someone you know is in one of the affected areas, or anywhere else dealing with unreliable water, here's what we found comparing four of them: what each one actually claims, and what real buyers say once they've used it.

Key takeaways

  • All four run on rechargeable batteries and pump water from any bucket, tank, or container you already have. None of them need a plumbing hookup.
  • Only one of the four, the GIRAVO, includes a built-in immersion heater to actually warm the water.
  • The cheapest option ($19.99) has strong reviews but at least one serious pump-failure report, worth knowing before you buy.
  • The SPACEREST is the only one that ships with its own 5-gallon water tank, so you're not improvising a bucket. But it also has the weakest review average of the four (3.4 stars) with recurring battery and pump-durability complaints.

Which portable shower is right for you?

These four solve slightly different problems, even though they look similar in a thumbnail. Here's how we'd sort them:

Aybvouy Portable Shower, best all-around value

What the listing says: A 6000mAh rechargeable battery rated for up to 90 minutes of continuous use, with USB fast charging. It has 4 adjustable spray modes and an IPX8 waterproof rating with a built-in filtration system to keep debris out of the pump. Amazon lists it at $28.88, down from a $59.99 list price. See it directly on Amazon.

What buyers are saying: This is the highest-rated of the four at 4.4 stars across 29 ratings, with 67% at 5 stars. Reviewers call out good water pressure and the LED display as genuinely useful, and mention using it for camping, washing pets, and rinsing off after the beach. It's not unanimous: one 3-star review says the pressure is “terrible by comparison” to other units they've tried, and there's a single 1-star complaint about weak power. But the pattern across reviews leans clearly positive.

GIRAVO Portable Shower, best for warm water

What the listing says: This is the one meaningful differentiator in the group. GIRAVO includes a 1500W immersion heater built into the kit specifically to warm the water before use, on top of the same 6000mAh battery class as the others (rated 120 to 150 minutes here). It also has an LED display for battery and water temperature, and multiple mounting options: handheld, hook, or suction cup. Listed at $34.99, down from $69.99. See it directly on Amazon.

What buyers are saying: Both existing reviews are 5 stars, praising fast warm-up time and the shower head quality. That said, two ratings is a thin sample, not enough to call it a proven favorite yet, just an early positive signal on the one feature none of the others offer.

Budget pick, $19.99 portable shower (Eyomo)

What the listing says: Same 6000mAh battery class as Aybvouy, rated for 90 minutes on high or 120 minutes on low, with 3 water modes and a claimed 8L/min max flow. It charges from a phone charger, power bank, laptop, or a 12V car socket, and has a removable bottom filter. At $19.99 against a $39.99 list price, it's the cheapest of the four by a wide margin. See it directly on Amazon.

What buyers are saying: 4.3 stars across 8 ratings, with mentions of it holding up for dog-washing, camping, and even field use by a search-and-rescue K9 handler. The one real flag: a 2-star review reports the pump flooding internally and failing in under two hours, which the reviewer suspected was a defective seal. With only 8 ratings total, one bad unit carries more weight in the average, worth knowing going in, especially at this price point where you're already trading off some margin for error.

SPACEREST Portable Shower, best all-in-one setup

What the listing says: The SPACEREST's real advantage isn't battery life. It's that the pump, lid, and a 5-gallon food-grade water jug are one integrated unit, so you don't need to bring your own bucket or container at all. It runs on a 4000mAh battery for about 80 minutes, has an LED display, and packs down into an included mesh bag with 7 spray modes. Listed at $41.50, down from $82.99. See it directly on Amazon.

What buyers are saying: This is the one place the numbers don't line up as cleanly with the pitch. SPACEREST sits at 3.4 stars across 52 ratings, the lowest of the four, with a full 25% at 1 star. The recurring complaint isn't the tank, it's the battery: multiple reviewers report the pump failing outright after 60 to 90 days, and note there's no way to buy a replacement battery separately, meaning a dead battery means replacing the whole unit. If the built-in tank is genuinely what you need and you don't want to improvise a container, it's still the only one of the four that offers it. Just go in aware of the durability pattern other buyers have flagged.

Bottom line

None of these four is a bad product on paper, the specs cluster close together, which is exactly why the reviews matter more than the listing copy. If you want the safest, most broadly-liked option, Aybvouy is it. If warm water matters to you and you're comfortable being an early adopter on a thin review count, GIRAVO's heater is a real feature none of the others have. If you're working with a tight budget, the $19.99 option has good reviews but a real failure report to weigh in. And if not having to hunt down a bucket matters more than anything else, SPACEREST is the only one built that way. Just know its track record on durability is the weakest of the group.

If you're one of the people dealing with this right now, on the island or anywhere else, I hope this actually helps, even in a small way.
