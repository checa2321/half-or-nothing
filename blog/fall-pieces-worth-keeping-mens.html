A guide to dress shoe styles

This edit spans two shoe families. Here is what to look for, starting with what is above.

  • Penny loafer - a plain strap across the vamp, sometimes with a coin slot. The Antonio Maurizi beige suede takes this shape.
  • Tassel loafer - a penny loafer's strap replaced by a pair of hanging tassels. Both the black and brown Antonio Maurizi pairs here are tassel loafers.
  • Bit loafer - a metal bar or buckle set across the vamp, the shape Gucci and Ferragamo made famous. The grey Ferragamo above is a bit loafer.
  • Venetian loafer - the plainest version, a clean seam with no strap, tassel or hardware at all.
  • Horsebit loafer - a specific bit loafer with a metal snaffle-bit shape, the original Gucci design.
  • Oxford - the most formal lace-up. The eyelet tabs are stitched under the vamp, so the front closes flat. The Brioni and both Johnston & Murphy Uptons above are oxfords.
  • Derby - a more relaxed lace-up. The eyelet tabs sit on top of the vamp, which is why it flexes and fits easier than an Oxford.
  • Monk strap - no laces at all, just one or two buckled straps across the vamp. A single monk is quieter; a double monk is the more fashion-forward version.
  • Brogue - not a shoe on its own, but a decoration: the small perforated holes punched along the seams of an Oxford, Derby or wingtip toe, like the black Johnston & Murphy above.
  • Chukka boot - an ankle boot with two or three lace eyelets and an open throat, originally worn by polo players.
  • Chelsea boot - an ankle boot with no laces, held on by an elastic panel at the side instead.
