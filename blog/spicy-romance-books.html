What Does "Spicy" Mean?

Spicy is not a genre as such. It is a term readers use for books of any genre with highly erotic scenes and plenty of sexual tension, as Planeta de Libros explains, in Spanish. It took off on BookTok. Readers share their favorite spicy reads there and often rate them with hot pepper emojis on a scale of one to five, as CBC News reported.

There is no official rating, so here is how I use the scale: one chili is mostly tension and kisses, and five is as explicit as it gets. Every book below shows my own rating. For the books I have not read yet, it is my best estimate from what readers say about them. Want a second opinion on heat? Reader sites like Romance.io and StoryGraph show how other readers rate each book.

The Three Worlds in This Post

  • Contemporary romance: love stories set in the real world, today, per this glossary.
  • Romantasy: a subgenre that blends fantasy and romance, as Merriam-Webster defines it. A New York Times writer quoted on that page describes it as mixing the passion and spice of romance with the escapism and high-stakes drama of fantasy.
  • Dark romance: a subgenre that explores the angsty, and sometimes taboo, side of love, often with morally grey characters, obsessive relationships and high emotional stakes, according to Penguin. It can carry heavy themes, so check the content warnings before you start.

The Tropes You Will See Here

A trope is a narrative device with specific characteristics that keeps working with readers, as Planeta de Libros explains, in Spanish. These are the ones that show up in this post:

  • Enemies to lovers: the main characters start out as enemies or on opposite sides before they fall for each other.
  • Slow burn: a long build-up before the romantic or sexual payoff.
  • Grumpy and sunshine: one lead is surly and closed off, the other is happy and outgoing.
  • Fake relationship: the leads pretend to be together and the pretending becomes real.
  • Forced proximity: two people have to spend a lot of time together or live together for reasons they cannot control.
  • Marriage of convenience: a marriage made for practical reasons that turns into love.
  • Brother's best friend: a thin line between being your brother's best friend and something more.
  • Forbidden love: a romance that is not supposed to happen.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "spicy" mean in romance books?

Spicy is not a genre as such. It is a term readers use for books of any genre with highly erotic scenes and plenty of sexual tension, as Planeta de Libros explains, in Spanish. It took off on BookTok, where readers often rate spicy reads with hot pepper emojis on a scale of one to five, as CBC News reported.

What is the difference between clean and open door romance?

In a clean or wholesome romance there are no sex scenes on the page, only romance and kisses. In an open door romance you will see sex on the page. Spicy or steamy books sit at the open end, where sex features heavily, as The Romance Genre Specialist describes it.

eBay. Rescanned hourly. Sorted purely by discount, and nothing under 50% is listed. No countdown timers, no invented scarcity: every number on this page is measured, not staged. See How We Verify Deals for the full methodology.

Amazon works differently. The Amazon Picks section is hand-picked by Che, one at a time. When a pick shows a price, list price, or coupon, it was verified by hand directly on Amazon -- not pulled from an automated scan -- see About for why.

Affiliate disclosure & how this page is built
Some links on this site are affiliate links. This site is a member of the eBay Partner Network. If you buy through one, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Commission never affects which deals are listed or the order they appear in. Listings default to newest first; sort by discount, price, or name any time. Original prices and discount percentages are set by the seller and shown as reported by the retailer's API; Half or Nothing does not independently verify them. As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.
Pricing & how often this updates

Prices and availability change at the retailer. Whatever the retailer shows at the time of purchase is what applies.

The "% off" shown on each card is the discount the seller lists against their own stated original price. It comes straight from the retailer's data, not from our own price history, and we don't independently verify it.
