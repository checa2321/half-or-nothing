Dark Spots: How to Build a Simple Routine for More Even Skin

What are dark spots?

Dark spots, also called hyperpigmentation, are patches of skin that have made extra melanin. The most common type is post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, which is the mark left behind after a breakout, a bug bite, a scrape, or any other irritation heals. Sun exposure creates its own spots over time and darkens the ones you already have.

The American Academy of Dermatology notes that these marks are usually harmless and that treating them takes patience, often months and sometimes longer, with consistency mattering more than any single product. Visible light, the kind that comes through a window or off a screen, can also make existing spots worse, according to the Skin Cancer Foundation, which is why the sunscreen step below is not optional.

One safety note before the routine: if a spot changes shape or color, appears suddenly, bleeds, or does not improve with care, see a dermatologist so it can be checked properly.

The first product to buy: sunscreen

If you only change one thing, make it a daily broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher. It protects the spots you have from getting darker and gives the rest of the routine a chance to work.

For dark spots specifically, a tinted mineral sunscreen is worth the small upgrade. The iron oxides that give it color also add a layer of protection against visible light. A tinted formula doubles as a light base, so it can replace a separate step in the morning.

How to build a simple morning and night routine

Keep it to a few steps and do them every day. That is the whole trick.

Morning

1. Cleanse with a gentle cleanser. 2. Apply a brightening serum, like vitamin C or niacinamide. 3. Moisturize with a lightweight cream. 4. Finish with broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher. If you can, pick a tinted one with iron oxides. It also helps protect against visible light, which can make dark spots worse.

Night

1. Remove makeup and cleanse gently. 2. Apply your dark spot treatment, as tolerated. 3. Moisturize to support your skin barrier.

Frequently asked questions

How long do dark spots take to fade?

Usually a few months, and sometimes a year or more for deeper ones. Daily sunscreen and a consistent routine matter more than any single product.

Does sunscreen really help with dark spots?

Yes. Sun and visible light darken existing spots and create new ones. A daily broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher, ideally tinted with iron oxides, is the step that makes the rest of the routine work.

Can I use vitamin C and retinol together?

Most people use vitamin C or niacinamide in the morning and retinol at night rather than layering them at once. Start slow with the retinol and skip a night if your skin feels irritated.

Is hydroquinone safe to use?

Hydroquinone can be effective but is best used under a dermatologist's guidance. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has warned that over-the-counter skin-lightening products with hydroquinone are not recognized as safe and effective and can cause side effects. The options in this guide are gentler starting points.

What if my dark spots are not improving?

If a spot changes, appears suddenly, or does not respond to a consistent routine over a few months, see a dermatologist.
