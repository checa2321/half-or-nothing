Where to Buy Discounted Fashion Clothing (50% Off or More, Verified Live)

Published 2026-09-15 · Updated 2026-09-24 · Written by Checarlos Cruz · 9 min read

Most "fashion sales" online aren't sales. A 30% markdown on a dress that was quietly listed at double its usual price two weeks ago isn't a deal, it's a label. The question that actually matters isn't "is this on sale?" It's "is this genuinely half the normal price?"

Set the bar there and almost every source drops out. Retail stores go that deep only in short clearance windows. Brand sites almost never do. This guide covers the channels that consistently clear 50% on real clothing, what's live in our own inventory right now, and the checks that matter specifically for fashion, not electronics or general merchandise.

Which Channels Actually Produce 50%+ Clothing Discounts

Individual eBay Sellers

Clothing resale on eBay comes from people and small liquidators clearing closets, estate sales, and overstock pallets, not from a retailer trying to look generous. A seller who paid $40 for a jacket and lists it at $18 isn't running a promotion. They want it gone. That's why eBay produces real 50%, 60%, even 90% off clothing at volume, while brand sites almost never do.

Retail Clearance, With One Catch

Real end-of-season clearance exists, and it's genuine when a retailer needs floor space for the next season's stock. The catch is the "original price" printed on the tag. Harvard Business School research found that for every $1 increase in a posted "original price," shoppers were willing to pay an average of 77 cents more, which is exactly why some outlet and clearance tags carry a number the item was never actually sold at. A real clearance discount off a real price is honest. A discount off an invented anchor isn't, even if the item is genuinely cheap.

Outlet and "Made for Outlet" Stock

This one is specific to apparel. Consumers' Checkbook's 2026 investigation found that 80 to 85 percent of the goods sold at outlet and factory stores were manufactured specifically for those off-price locations, not leftover stock from regular retail, according to marketing professor Gonca Soysal. Those items get tagged with a fictitious "original retail" price that was never charged anywhere, since the item never existed at full price to begin with. The discount looks real. The anchor isn't. This trick barely exists in electronics or home goods; it's largely a clothing and footwear phenomenon.

The practical takeaway: for clothing specifically, eBay's individual-seller listings are the most consistently honest 50%+ source, because the person setting the price has no marketing incentive to inflate what came before it.

Reading a Clothing Listing Correctly

Fashion resale carries different risk than electronics. Nobody's worried a dress is "for parts, not working." The two things that actually go wrong are fit and fabrication, not function.

NWT Is the Signal That Matters Most

"New With Tags" means the item was purchased, never worn, and still carries the original retail tag. It's the closest thing fashion resale has to "sealed in box." A listing marked NWT with a clear tag photo is the safest tier of clothing purchase you can make secondhand, because there's no wear to misjudge and no fit history to guess at.

Check the Brand's Actual Size Chart, Not the Tag Number

A size 10 in one brand runs nothing like a size 10 in another. Before buying, pull up the brand's own size chart (most are one search away) and compare it to the measurements listed on the item, not just the number on the tag. This is the single most common reason a great-looking 60%-off dress ends up returned.

Watch for the Anchor-Price Version of the Same Trick, Even in a Filtered Feed

The 50% floor filters out most fake-discount theater, but it isn't a fabrication detector on its own. A listing from an unfamiliar label showing a jump from a four-figure "original price" to a $30-40 sale price deserves the same cross-check you'd apply anywhere: search the brand name plus the item, and see whether that original price shows up anywhere else it was actually charged. Most of the time it will. When it doesn't, that's worth knowing before you buy, not after.

When Clothing Actually Goes 50%+ Off

Fashion discounting runs on a seasonal clock that doesn't apply to electronics:

  • Swimwear clears hardest in late July through August, once summer inventory has to move before it's a year old.
  • Coats and outerwear clear in January and February, right after the cold snap that was the last real selling window.
  • Sweaters and knitwear clear a bit later than coats, typically March, once the heaviest winter buying is over but the weather hasn't fully turned.
  • Formalwear and occasion dresses see a real dip in early January, after the holiday and New Year's event season ends.
  • Back-to-school apparel clears in late September through October, once the actual back-to-school buying window has closed and retailers need the floor space for holiday stock.
  • Denim and basics discount more evenly year-round, since they're not tied to a single season the way swimwear or coats are.

Buying a category right after its selling season ends is the single most reliable way to catch a genuine 50%+ clothing discount, on top of anything a filtered feed already surfaces.

What Rarely Hits 50% in Fashion

  • Core, brand-name denim (the kind that rarely goes on sale even at full retail) holds its price on resale too.
  • Licensed collaboration pieces and limited drops trade at a premium, not a discount, once the initial release sells out.
  • Already-cheap fast-fashion basics have little room to fall 50%, since the starting price was low to begin with.

Buying and Sizing on eBay Fashion

eBay's Money Back Guarantee covers most purchases if an item never arrives or doesn't match the listing description, same as any other category. The difference with clothing is that "doesn't match the listing" almost never means the item is broken. It usually means the fit is wrong. Read the actual measurements in the description, not just the size label, and check whether the seller accepts returns for fit before you buy anything without a clear size chart in the photos.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the clothing discounts on Half or Nothing verified?

Yes. Every listing is checked against the seller's own original list price at the time it's published, and expired or sold-out listings get pulled rather than left up as stale history.

What does "NWT" mean on a clothing listing?

New With Tags. The item was bought, never worn, and still has the original manufacturer tag attached. It's the lowest-risk condition tier in clothing resale, since there's no wear or fit history to account for.

Why do sport coats and swimwear discount deeper than jeans on average?

Sport coats and swimwear are more seasonal and carry higher original markups, which leaves more room for a genuine deep markdown once the selling window closes. Denim pricing tends to be closer to honest year-round, so there's less inflated anchor to cut through.

Is it safe to buy clothing secondhand on eBay?

Yes, with the same basic checks as any eBay purchase: seller feedback score and recency, a clear return policy, and actual photos rather than stock images. For clothing specifically, also check the brand's real size chart against the listed measurements before buying.
