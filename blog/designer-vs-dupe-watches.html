The Neo-Vintage Case Size Shift

There's a second reason this category of watch is having a moment beyond price. After years of oversized cases, the industry itself is shrinking back toward 36mm to 39mm proportions. Watch media calls this look "neo-vintage." Several icons on this list, Cartier Tank alternatives especially, were always built compact. That means the affordable side of this list has been quietly on-trend the whole time, not playing catch-up.

This is also exactly where an affordable homage hits its real ceiling. Building a mechanical movement thin enough to disappear on the wrist without losing power reserve or shock resistance is one of the hardest, most expensive problems in watchmaking. That is why the "quiet luxury" flat profile you see on a $12,000 dress watch is one detail a $50 quartz homage genuinely can't fake. It's a fair tradeoff to know about, not a reason to skip the category.

What a Dupe Actually Is (and Isn't)

A dupe (or homage) watch is a real, independently sold watch that echoes a famous design without copying its name or logo. A dupe in watches is different from a dupe in handbags. Nobody is stamping a fake Cartier logo on a Casio. What's happening instead is that a handful of design ideas became so iconic that dozens of legitimate watchmakers built their own versions under their own name. Think of a rectangular case with Roman numerals, or an octagonal bezel over an integrated bracelet. Reviewers call these "homages." Some, like the Swatch MoonSwatch, are literally an official collaboration with the original brand. Others, however, are simply an independent brand's honest take on a shape that isn't patented. For a deeper look at how our own live inventory sorts real Swiss names from everyday workhorse brands, see our brand guide to the watches on Half or Nothing. Also, if you're still deciding between a sport chronograph and a slim dress watch, we broke that choice down in Chronograph vs Dress Watch: Which One Should You Buy at Half Off.

This is also different from buying an illegal replica. A superclone or AAA fake tries to pass itself off as the real designer product, logo included. That comes with real downsides beyond the ethics: unstable modified movements no legitimate watchmaker will service, fake sapphire crystal, and water resistance that exists on paper only. A homage from Seiko, Citizen, or Timex is the opposite. It's sold under its own name, it holds its own resale value, and if something breaks, any watchmaker can actually fix it.

One more reason this category is having a real moment right now: sphere color. A "Tiffany blue" or pastel dial can turn an otherwise ordinary $70 watch into something people actively hunt for, the same way a specific colorway drives sneaker resale. The Casio MTP-1302PD below is the clearest example of this on our list.

There's a real legal line on the used-watch side too, and it's worth knowing before you ever shop vintage. In a 2019 lawsuit, Rolex argued that swapping non-Rolex parts such as dials and crystals onto a genuine Rolex makes the result a counterfeit, even though the watch started out authentic. WatchPro reported that case as a landmark test, and we did not check how it ended. None of the products on this list have that problem, because none of them are wearing a logo they didn't earn.

IWC Big Pilot and Portugieser

IWC's pilot watch lineage goes back to a real wartime requirement, and its dress-watch cousin, the Portugieser, has its own affordable answer, at least on paper.

Not currently in our inventory: the Timex Fairfield Chronograph, directly cited alongside the Portugieser for sharing its 41mm proportions. We had this one verified live earlier the same day this post was written, at 60.8% off. Then it sold out before publishing. That is proof that even a documented real dupe can disappear between research and print. We're watching for it to restock.

Also worth knowing about: Laco, a German brand with a real historical claim most homages can't make. In 1940, Germany's Air Ministry issued a technical specification for a navigation watch, the B-Uhr, calling for a 55mm case, a crown built for use with gloves, and a chronometer-grade movement. Only five manufacturers built it: IWC, A. Lange & Söhne, Stowa, Wempe, and Laco. Monochrome notes that Lange and Laco used in-house German movements, while Wempe and Stowa used Swiss ones and IWC built its own caliber. Laco still builds pilot watches directly descended from that same specification today, which is the same design lineage IWC's own Big Pilot line claims, at a small fraction of the price.

Omega Speedmaster "Moonwatch"

The first watch worn on the Moon carries a chronograph history few brands can compete with. One of its most respected budget alternatives, however, has its own real space story.

Bulova Lunar Pilot Not just a look-alike. Bulova's own chronograph technically has lunar surface heritage of its own.

  • Movement: Quartz chronograph
  • Style: Space-heritage chronograph

On August 2, 1971, Apollo 15 astronaut Dave Scott was wearing his NASA-issued Omega Speedmaster when its Hesalite crystal popped off during his second moonwalk. For his third and final moonwalk, Scott wore a Bulova chronograph he had personally agreed to test for the company. NASA later referred to it as an "unauthorized timepiece" and kept its brand name out of official records. Scott's Omegas from the earlier walks eventually went to the Smithsonian. His Bulova stayed in private hands for decades, until it sold at auction in 2015 for $1.62 million. It is the only privately owned watch to have walked on the Moon.

Rolex Submariner

The Submariner is the watch every dive watch since 1953 has quietly answered to. Its black dial, its geometric hour markers, its rotating bezel, all of it has been referenced so many times that "Submariner homage" is its own category in watch reviews.

Invicta Pro Diver (Automatic) The line reviewers reach for first when they want the Submariner look with a real automatic movement inside.

  • Movement: Automatic, Seiko-supplied NH35 caliber

One reviewer at Two Broke Watch Snobs describes the Pro Diver 8926OB as a classic 40mm watch with a Submariner case that doesn't break the bank. Frequently Asked Questions

Is buying a homage watch the same as buying a fake? No. A fake copies a brand's actual name and logo. A homage is built and sold under its own brand name, openly referencing a design that isn't patented, the same way countless car designs or furniture silhouettes get reinterpreted across brands.

Why do some watches on this list have no price or purchase link? Half or Nothing only lists a product with a price when it's verified live in our own inventory at 50% off or more. Some of the watches above are real, well-documented homages we simply don't have in stock right now at that discount. We're keeping an eye on them.

What is the Seiko NH35 movement, and why does it matter? It's a real automatic watch movement launched in 2011 by Time Module Inc., a Seiko Instruments division. It's become the standard affordable automatic caliber across the watch industry, which is why an automatic homage watch under $150 can still be mechanically legitimate instead of just visually similar.

Which of these is the best starting point if I've never bought a homage watch before? There's no wrong entry point. However, the Cartier Tank category has the widest range of prices on this list. That makes it the easiest place to compare a $50 option against a $150 option side by side.
