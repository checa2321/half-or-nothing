What Does "Spicy" Mean?

Spicy is not a genre as such. It is a term readers use for books of any genre with highly erotic scenes and plenty of sexual tension, as Planeta de Libros explains, in Spanish. It took off on BookTok. Readers share their favorite spicy reads there and often rate them with hot pepper emojis on a scale of one to five, as CBC News reported.

There is no official rating, so here is how I use the scale: one chili is mostly tension and kisses, and five is as explicit as it gets. Every book below shows my own rating, or says coming soon if I have not read it yet. Want a second opinion on heat? Reader sites like Romance.io and StoryGraph show how other readers rate each book.

Not every book in this post is spicy: some of them are mostly about the story, and their level is low on purpose. That is useful to know before you pick one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "spicy" mean in romance books?

Spicy is not a genre as such. It is a term readers use for books of any genre with highly erotic scenes and plenty of sexual tension, as Planeta de Libros explains, in Spanish. It took off on BookTok, where readers often rate spicy reads with hot pepper emojis on a scale of one to five, as CBC News reported.

What is the difference between clean and open door romance?

In a clean or wholesome romance there are no sex scenes on the page, only romance and kisses. In an open door romance you will see sex on the page. Spicy or steamy books sit at the open end, where sex features heavily, as The Romance Genre Specialist describes it.

How do I know what to expect before I start a book?

Every book in this post shows my own spice rating, from one to five chili peppers, or says coming soon if I have not read it yet, and a link to the content warnings readers report on StoryGraph. Check that list before you start, especially for dark romance and romantasy, which can carry heavy themes.
